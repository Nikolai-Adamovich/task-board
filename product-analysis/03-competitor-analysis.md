## 2. Why these products are structured this way (analysis)

- **Jira's configurability exists because its buyers are 500+ orgs needing compliance and heterogeneous processes.** The
  same four schemes (workflow, permission, screen, field) that enable ISO/SOX audits produce the "configuration debt"
  small teams drown in
  ([youngju.dev](https://www.youngju.dev/blog/culture/2026-05-16-project-management-issue-tracker-2026-linear-jira-asana-clickup-height-shortcut-plane-deep-dive.en)).
  Lesson: configurability must be bought with admin labor; a simplified tool should ship decisions instead.
- **Linear removes configuration to buy speed.** Fixed status vocabulary, atomic issues, auto-rolled cycles. Its
  trade-offs (no wiki, no deep permissions, occasional silent-conflict UX criticism on HN) show what a minimal tracker
  gives up. Lesson: adopt the defaults philosophy, keep explicit conflict handling (see
  [12-task-workflow.md](12-task-workflow.md)).
- **Asana multi-homes tasks across projects** because cross-functional work doesn't fit single ownership. We do NOT need
  this: our domain is software work inside one Project. Lesson: resist multi-project tasks; it doubles data-model
  complexity.
- **Trello proves simplicity wins adoption but loses engineering process** (no sprints/types/statuses discipline).
  Lesson: keep Trello-grade friction for the first five minutes, Jira-grade structure underneath.
- **YouTrack/GitLab/Azure DevOps tie issues to code.** Out of scope initially; integration is roadmap material.
- **GitHub Projects treats boards as saved views over issues** — conceptually identical to our Board-as-configuration
  decision, which validates it.

## 3. Lessons for our product

1. **Ship opinions, allow overrides.** Default types/statuses/board on Project creation (already decided) mirrors
   Linear's success formula while keeping our configurable entities.
2. **Decouple Status from Board Column** (already decided) — it fixes a documented Jira pain (P4) at near-zero cost.
3. **One active sprint is a convention, not a law.** Jira enforces one active sprint per board (unless parallel sprints
   enabled); Linear allows overlap implicitly. Our unrestricted sprint transitions deliberately permit flexibility — but
   the UI should still make "the active sprint" obvious (see [14-sprint-analysis.md](14-sprint-analysis.md)).
4. **Complete-sprint flow needs an unfinished-work decision.** Jira asks: move incomplete issues to Backlog / future
   sprint / new sprint. We should copy this dialog pattern (recommendation in
   [14-sprint-analysis.md](14-sprint-analysis.md)) even though our Sprint completion itself stays manual.
5. **Permissions: few roles, one matrix.** Every competitor with >4 layered permission systems generates confusion
   complaints. Our 5-role model is already at the upper bound of comfortable.
6. **Viewer/guest class is standard and monetizable** — validates read-only Viewer as final.
7. **Search: structured filters first, JQL never (initially).** JQL is loved by experts and feared by everyone else;
   URL-addressable structured filters give 90% of the value.
8. **Onboarding time-to-value is the adoption battleground.** Setup-time comparisons (15 min Linear vs 2–8 h Jira)
   correlate with the market shift toward dev-first tools.
9. **Avoid feature-count competition.** ClickUp demonstrates breadth degrades depth and clarity; our non-goals list
   protects this.
